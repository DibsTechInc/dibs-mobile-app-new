01

The price is on the card

Every row answers "what will this cost me" before it is tapped — Included · 10-Class Pass, or a dollar amount, or Waitlist. No one has to open a class to find out.

  • Capacity is stated, not implied. "3 spots left" appears at ≤3; above that the row simply says nothing, because a half-full class is not news.
  • Times are the studio's wall clock, printed verbatim. A 6:00 PM class reads 6:00 PM on a phone in any timezone — and the "is this in the past" maths runs through studioNow(), never the device clock.
  • Filters are chips, not a modal. One tap to narrow, one to undo.
  • Instructor names hide entirely for studios where that data is meaningless — a config flag, not a special case in the code.

02

The time is the headline

The Fraunces numeral is the largest thing on the screen. It is the fact the client is actually here to confirm, and setting it as a display moment rather than metadata is most of what makes this not look like gym software.

  • The cancellation window is stated in plain time, before booking. Not "12-hour policy" — "until 6:00 AM tomorrow". The studio's own configured notice period, rendered as a moment the client can picture.
  • What it costs is settled here: $0, and which pass pays for it. Same answer the schedule row gave.
  • One filled CTA on the screen. If a second action mattered as much, neither would.

03

The money moment

The best option is already chosen when the sheet opens. The client's job is to confirm, not to shop. Alternatives stay visible so the choice is theirs, but nobody is asked to pick from a list of one.

  • Preselection order mirrors the widget exactly: eligible pass → credit that covers the total → card. Same rules, same result, so the app and the web can never quote a client different prices.
  • The promo entry is a quiet text link. Present for the people who have a code, invisible to everyone else — a prominent empty field makes every client feel they are missing a discount.
  • A $0 total is always confirmable. Pass-covered, credit-covered, and comped bookings have no card by design.
  • The total is the server's number, not ours. Our own pricing maths is a display estimate; if the two ever disagree the server wins and the divergence is logged.

04

The theming stress test

This is the screen where Everyday Ballet's pink proves the system. The fill stays the studio's actual colour, the text on it flips to dark ink automatically because white would sit at 2.34:1, and the accent used as text becomes a deeper rose so it clears contrast on white. Three different values, one studio input.

  • The full breakdown is shown before the tap. Price, promo, tax, total — a client should never learn the real number from a receipt.
  • The amount is repeated on the button. "Confirm booking · $31.35" is the last thing under their thumb.
  • Apple Pay appears only once the studio's merchant ID is registered. Cards work regardless — the option is absent, never broken.

05

A designed moment, not an alert

The checkmark draws in over 400ms; nothing else moves. No confetti — the brief is explicit that manufactured excitement reads as cheap on a surface this calm.

  • The pass count updates in front of them: "6 left", down from 7. The client sees the consequence of what they just did without going to look for it.
  • "Add to calendar" is the one place we convert the stored time into a real instant, using the studio's timezone. Everywhere else the stored value is printed verbatim — get this wrong and the entry lands hours off.
  • This screen is what earns the push-notification permission ask later. We ask after a successful booking, never on first launch.

06

Say what they get back, before they decide

The sheet states the consequence in the client's own terms — "31 hours before" and "1 class back on your pass" — rather than quoting a policy. Late cancels get the same treatment with the opposite content: what they lose, in the same position.

  • Destructive is the danger colour, never the accent. A studio's brand colour must not come to mean "you are about to lose something".
  • "Keep my spot" is the calmer, easier button. Cancelling is available, not encouraged.
  • The early/late decision compares the class time against studioNow(). Using the device clock would be four to seven hours out for these studios — enough to charge somebody for an on-time cancel.

What the account is doing

The journey card is the only place the accent floods a surface, and it earns it by being the one emotional object on an otherwise administrative screen. The count is a Fraunces numeral because a number a client is proud of should be set like one.

  • "Delete account" is present and quiet. Apple requires it for any app with sign-up. Tucked at the bottom in the danger colour, and its confirmation says plainly what is kept for financial records and that an active membership must be cancelled first — rather than hiding it and hoping nobody looks.
  • The upcoming payment is stated as a date and an amount, framed around the 25th billing cycle. A subscription client should never be surprised by a charge.
  • Passes lead with the number remaining, large. It is the fact they open the wallet to check.
  • Rows are separated by hairlines, not wrapped in individual cards. Cards are for things that are interactive or genuinely need a boundary; a settings list is neither.
  • "Powered by Dibs" is the only Dibs mark anywhere in the app, set in tertiary text.
