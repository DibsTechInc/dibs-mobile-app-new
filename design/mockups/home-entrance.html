Home — the app-open sequence

The photograph now holds the screen on its own before the schedule rises under it. Same layout in all three; the only variable is how long that beat lasts. B is what is built. Replay each one several times — this fires on every launch, so the fifth viewing is the one that decides whether it is elegant or tiresome.

What else changed

It runs once per launch, not once per mount. Home rebuilds itself every time you come back to it from another screen, and it re-renders on pull-to-refresh. Left alone, this entrance would replay every one of those times — and motion that repeats on a screen you are moving through stops reading as arrival and starts reading as lag. So the sequence fires on the first Home of an app launch and never again until you relaunch. The hero keeps breathing either way; the drift is ambient, not an entrance, and stopping it would make the screen feel deader than it was a moment earlier.

The content rises whatever state it is in. Skeleton, error, or the real schedule — they all come up together at the same moment, so the sequence is the same length on a fast network and a slow one. The alternative, waiting for data before animating, makes the app's rhythm a function of the studio's wifi.

Reduce-motion turns all of it off. Someone who has asked their phone to stop moving things has asked us too. The content is simply there.
