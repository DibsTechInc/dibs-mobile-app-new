The whole picture, held, then the schedule arrives on top of it from below. B is what is built. The variants differ only in how long the photograph has the screen to itself. Replay each several times — this fires on every launch, so the fifth viewing decides whether it feels considered or slow.

Two things this makes true

The hero must now be composed for the FULL frame, with its subject high. What stays on screen at rest is the top 43% of a full-screen crop. Previously the photo was only ever shown in a 360px band and cropped to its centre, so the middle of the picture was what mattered. Now the whole thing is seen for a beat and then the top of it is what remains. The last frame shows the sequence with Carlsbad's real remote asset — a 1961×1258 landscape web banner — for comparison against the bundled vertical one.

The native splash is now the seam. Expo shows its own splash before any of this, so the sequence is really: splash → full-screen photo → panel. If the splash were the same photograph, the handoff would be invisible and the app would appear to open in one continuous move. Worth doing, and it is a one-line change per studio in app.config.ts — but Everyday Ballet has a designed wordmark splash you may want to keep, so it is your call rather than mine.
