The studio's app, not Dibs's

Second pass. Home is stripped back to a photograph and three choices — the thing the old app got right and this one had buried under seven. Icons are real Lucide at 1.5px stroke, and schedule rows align to the top so a time never floats beside a two-line class name.

Fixed since the last pass

  • Home was doing too much. Greeting, subtitle, section label, two bordered cards, a CTA and four tabs — seven things on one photograph, all competing with it. Now: greeting, subtitle, three choices.
  • Today's schedule is gone from Home. Someone opening a booking app already knows what they came for; a day's classes on the way in is a second schedule to scroll past.
  • The icons were unicode glyphs — ▤ ◇ ○ ⌂. They rendered as striped rectangles and bare circles because that is what those characters are. Replaced with Lucide paths.
  • Schedule rows were centre-aligned, so a time drifted down beside a name that wrapped to three lines. Now top-aligned, and the time no longer breaks across two lines.
  • Packages is the widget's shopping-bag, so the two products agree. The cart became a trolley, since the bag was doing both jobs and they are not the same thing.
  • The tab bar is gone. One navigation model — three choices on Home, a back chevron on inner screens, the drawer for everything else.

Home — a photograph and three choices

Nothing else. Roughly three quarters of the screen is untouched photograph, which is what lets the picture and the menu read as one object rather than two competing ones. The three choices are joined by hairlines so they are a single menu, not a strip of buttons — and there is no "Home" among them, because the photograph is home.

What I removed, and why

Today's schedule. Your call, and the right one — it was a second schedule in front of the real one.

The upcoming-class cards. The idea was sound, the execution was not: bordered boxes on a photograph always fight it. "My Calendar" answers "when am I next in?" one tap away, which is where that question belongs.

The Home tab. Three choices, joined by hairlines. A four-tab bar makes the photo a backdrop behind an app; a menu makes it the surface the app sits on.

Schedule — the studio's colour, structurally

The coloured header block is the move worth keeping from the old app: it says whose app you are in without a logo, and it gives the day strip somewhere to live. Rows align to the top — the time's cap-height sits level with the class name's, whatever the name does below it.

No tab bar anywhere — settled

One navigation model, not two. Home's three choices push a screen with a back chevron, and the hamburger drawer is how you move sideways. That is what the old app did, and it is the reason Home felt uncluttered: nothing persistent competes for the bottom of the screen, so the photograph keeps the space a tab bar would have taken.

It also removes a seam. A bar that is absent on Home and present everywhere else announces itself at the first tap; this way the chrome is the same shape on every screen.

The trade to watch: Packages and My Calendar are now two taps from Home rather than one (open the drawer, then choose). For the surfaces people reach for daily — Book, and their own classes — the three choices still make it one tap, so the cost falls on the pages visited least.

Account — money first

Credit balance and passes above navigation, the way the old app had it. It is what people open the account for, and it answers "can I book?" before they go looking.
