The studio's app, not Dibs's

A revision after seeing it on a device. The old app got two big things right that this one lost: the photograph was the whole screen, and the studio's colour was structural rather than a garnish. Everything below follows from putting those back.

What went wrong

  • The hero band cut the picture in half. A 360px band over a full-screen crop sliced the dancer through the arm and face. The old app never had this problem because the photo was the screen.
  • The scrim read as a grey smudge. A gradient that ramps to full strength and stops mid-photograph draws a line across it. On a high-key image it is the first thing you see.
  • Two real layout bugs on Schedule — a ~400px void (a horizontal scroller with no height constraint expands to fill a column), and a day strip that runs …18 · 19 · 2 · 8… across a month boundary with no month label.
  • No tab bar, so the only way in was a small "SIGN IN" over the photo. The old app's three tabs are why it was easy to navigate.
  • "Welcome" where the old app said "Hi Alicia! Welcome to Everyday Ballet."

Home — the photograph is the screen

Full bleed, edge to edge, nothing cropping it. An even wash across the whole frame carries white type without ever drawing an edge, and the foot deepens only where words sit. The client's own classes come forward — that is what they open the app for.

Why this fixes it

The photo is never resized or cropped by a panel. The single reason the old app's home always looked right is that nothing sat on top of the image except type. Whatever the studio supplies, it is shown whole.

One even wash, not a ramp. A gradient that reaches full strength partway down draws a horizontal edge across the picture. A flat 34% veil over the entire frame plus a deeper foot has no edge to see.

The upcoming classes are the point. A member opens this app to answer "when am I next in?" — so that is what is on screen, in glass cards that sit on the photograph without hiding it.

Four tabs, always there. Packages is greyed until P4 ships rather than absent, so the bar does not change shape later.

Schedule — the studio's colour, structurally

The old app's coloured header block is the move worth keeping: it tells you whose app you are in without a logo, and it gives the day strip somewhere to live. The month label is not decoration — it is what stops 19 · 2 reading as a bug.

What the header earns

It says whose app this is without a logo lockup taking up room. A pink header and a slate-blue header are unmistakably two different studios running the same software — which is the whole promise of a white-label build.

The month label fixes the strip. On device it currently reads …18 · 19 · 2 · 8 · 9, which looks broken and is not — those are September dates. Naming the month, and keeping empty days present but dimmed, makes the sequence legible.

The list is dense on purpose. A schedule is scanned, not read. Time on the left as a fixed rail, name bold, everything else quiet, and the action always in the same place on the right.

"Included" replaces a price when a pass covers the class — but only once P2's pass data and the coverage rule agree, which they now do. That label is the thing the widget got wrong for months.

Account — money first

The old app put Credit Balance and Current Package at the very top, above navigation. That is right: it is what people open the account for, and it answers "can I book?" before they have to go looking.

Changes from what is built

Passes move up here from a separate wallet. Two taps to see whether you have a class left is one tap too many. The wallet screen stays for cards and history; the balances live where you land.

The numerals are the studio's colour. In an app whose whole personality is one colour, the money is the right place to spend it.

Still deliberately absent: the journey card (needs a lifetime class count nothing returns yet), referrals, and Delete account — which Apple requires before submission and which backend 7.7 has to build first.

What I would change from the old app, not copy

  • "Credit back +$0.00" on every row. It appeared on all of them and was $0.00 every time — a column of noise that trains people to ignore the money area. Show it only when there is credit back.
  • The truncated class names ("Replay BALLET DAN…", "Carlsbad Village Yoga…"). Repeating the studio's name on every row of that studio's own app costs the width the class name needed.
  • The flat colour block was heavier than it needs to be. Same idea, less height, no drop shadow, and the day strip inside it rather than stacked below.
  • Fraunces for times and numerals. The old app was all system sans. Keeping the serif for numbers is what stops this reading like a generic booking template — it is the one piece of the Dibs design DNA a studio app should keep.
